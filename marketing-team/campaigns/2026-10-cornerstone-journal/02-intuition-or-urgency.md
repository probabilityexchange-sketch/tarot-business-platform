# Intuition or Urgency? How to Tell Which Feeling Is Talking

One of the hardest parts of making an important decision is that every strong feeling wears the same coat at first glance.

You wake up at three in the morning with a sudden certainty that you need to send a resignation letter, end an agreement, or move across the state by Friday. Your heart is beating fast. The thought feels loud, insistent, and completely convincing.

In a culture that loves dramatic turning points, it is tempting to call any loud feeling *intuition*. We are often told to "trust our gut," as though the gut only ever speaks with one voice.

But anyone who has lived through a career change, a loss, or a long season of uncertainty knows that the inner life is a crowded room. A strong feeling can certainly be intuition. It can also be fear trying to protect you from disappointment. It can be pride wanting the last word. Or it can simply be exhaustion, the human wish to make a complicated question stop itching by choosing *anything* right now.

Discernment is the practice of sitting still long enough to notice which of those voices has the microphone.

## The Speed of Urgency vs. The Pace of Knowing

When I look at the Major Arcana alongside my own notebooks, two cards always remind me of this difference: The Moon and The High Priestess.

In The Moon, everything is lit by half-light. There is a winding path, a dog, a wolf, and water at your feet. In that kind of dim light, a bush by the side of the road looks exactly like a bear. When we are standing in a Moon season, when the facts aren't all in yet and we feel tired or exposed, urgency loves to step forward and demand an immediate answer.

Urgency almost always sounds like a countdown. It says: *If you don't decide tonight, you'll miss your chance. Send the message now. Close the door before you have to feel uncertain for one more afternoon.* Urgency hates waiting because waiting requires staying in the room with discomfort.

The High Priestess, by contrast, is seated still between two pillars with a scroll in her lap. She isn't running toward the exit.

When a feeling is rooted in genuine inner knowing, it rarely needs to shout or threaten you with a deadline. Even when the truth it points to is difficult, such as recognizing that a role no longer fits or that a boundary needs to be drawn, a quiet knowing can survive a night's sleep. If it is true on Tuesday at midnight, it will still be true on Thursday morning after breakfast.

## Four Questions to Separate the Voices

The next time a strong feeling arrives and demands that you take a big step immediately, you do not have to argue with it or talk yourself out of it. Instead, invite it to sit down at the table and ask four plain questions on paper.

### 1. What happens to this feeling if I give it forty-eight hours?
Unless the house is literally on fire, very few meaningful life decisions expire in two days. Notice how the feeling reacts when you say, *I hear you, and we will look at this on Saturday morning.* If the feeling panics at the thought of a two-day pause, you are almost certainly listening to urgency or fear rather than steady discernment.

### 2. What is this feeling trying to spare me from?
Every protective voice in us has a job. Sometimes the impulse to quit early is trying to spare us the vulnerability of asking for what we actually want. Sometimes the impulse to say "yes" immediately is trying to spare us the awkwardness of disappointing someone. Ask on the page: *If I acted on this impulse right now, what uncomfortable conversation or uncertainty would I get to skip?*

### 3. Does this feeling leave room for the whole picture?
Urgency loves all-or-nothing statements. It tells you that everything you built is wasted, or that one single change will solve every ache in your week. Quiet knowing is much more honest about complexity. It can say, *I am grateful for what this chapter gave me, and I also know it is time to begin preparing for the next one.*

### 4. What is the smallest reversible step I could take?
You rarely need to leap off the cliff of Card Zero all at once. If a feeling is pointing toward a real shift, test it with a small, ordinary step. Draft the email in a notebook where no one can see it. Have one honest conversation with a mentor. Pricing out the practical details in daylight often tells you more than three weeks of midnight rumination.
