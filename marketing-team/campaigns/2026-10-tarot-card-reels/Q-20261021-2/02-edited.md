# Major Arcana script pack: cards 0-10

## Series notes

**Shared structure (every video):**
1. HOOK, spoken in the first 2 seconds, with the on-screen text version.
2. SCRIPT: what the card often shows, what it traditionally points to, then how to reflect on it. Never a prediction.
3. CLOSING QUESTION, spoken and held on screen as text for the last few seconds.

**CTA line:** every TikTok and Facebook caption ends "Free guide in bio." (the free three-part discernment guide at kalimeister.com/letters). No booking mention, no prices, nothing else. Hashtags sit on their own line under the TikTok caption. YouTube Shorts titles carry no CTA (70-character limit); put "Free guide in bio." in the Shorts description instead.

**For Kali:**
- Card imagery is described as "often shown as..." because the deck is not chosen yet. Hold up whatever card you use when you say that line.
- Lines that start "I read it as..." are a starting point for your own take. Change any of them so they sound like how you actually read the card.
- Script word counts are 81-99. With the hook and closing question, each video runs roughly 40-50 seconds at a relaxed pace.
- Reversed meanings are left out on purpose to keep each video to one idea.

## 0. The Fool

- **Working title:** The step before you're sure
- **HOOK:** "The Major Arcana opens at the edge of a cliff."
- **ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The first card is standing at a cliff edge
- **SCRIPT (81 words):**
  This is The Fool, card zero. It's often shown as a traveler at the edge of that cliff, a small bag over one shoulder, a dog close by.
  Traditionally, it points to beginnings, openness, and taking a step before you have the whole map.
  It can also point to carelessness, so I read it as a question about how you're standing at your own edge.
  Are you curious? Are you rushing? Or are you waiting to feel completely sure first?
- **CLOSING QUESTION:** "Where are you standing at the edge of something right now?"
- **SHOT NOTES:** Kali to camera, waist-up, natural light. Card shown to camera on "often shown as"; closing question as on-screen text over the last shot.
- **TIKTOK CAPTION:** The Fool is a card about how you stand at a beginning. Curious, rushing, or waiting to be sure? Free guide in bio.

- **YOUTUBE SHORTS TITLE:** The Fool tarot card meaning: beginnings and the edge

## 1. The Magician

- **Working title:** What's already on the table
- **HOOK:** "Everything this card needs is already on the table."
- **ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** It's already on the table
- **SCRIPT (92 words):**
  The Magician is often shown standing at a table with four objects in front of them: a cup, a sword, a wand and a coin. Those are the four suits of the tarot. One hand points up, the other points down.
  Traditionally, it's a card about skill, focus and picking up the tools already in front of you instead of waiting for new ones.
  The shadow side is all talk and no follow-through.
  So when this card comes up, I look at the table first. What's already there that I keep overlooking?
- **CLOSING QUESTION:** "What's on your table that you haven't picked up yet?"

- **HOOK:** "Some cards talk. The High Priestess mostly listens."
- **ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The card that listens
- **SCRIPT (87 words):**
  The High Priestess is often shown seated between two pillars, one dark and one light, with a veil behind her and a scroll or book in her lap. She's sitting still with what she knows.
  Traditionally, she points to intuition, inner knowing, and the things you sense before you can explain them.

## Notes for reviewer

- **Assumptions:**
  - "Free guide in bio." assumes the bio link on each platform points to kalimeister.com/letters. Facebook Pages and profiles don't have a "bio" in the TikTok sense, so the line may need to read "Free guide at the link on my page" there. Kept verbatim per the Work Order.
  - Run time: scripts are 81-99 words. With the hook and closing question, each runs about 40-50 seconds.
  - Hashtags are on a separate line under the TikTok caption so the caption itself still ends with the CTA. TikTok caption text (without hashtags) is under 150 characters on every card.
- **Interpretation choices for Kali to weigh in on (most important first):**
  1. **Strength (8):** card order differs by tradition. Rider-Waite-style decks put Strength at 8 and Justice at 11. Marseille-style decks swap them. If the chosen deck uses Marseille order, Strength moves to pack 2 and Justice comes here. The script never says the number aloud, so only the heading and posting order change.
  2. **The Lovers (6):** framed as a values and choice card more than a romance card. Imagery differs a lot between decks, so the script names both common layouts.
  3. **The Hierophant (5):** the card shows a religious figure. The script stays neutral (tradition as "a root system" or "a habit nobody questions") and focuses on chosen versus inherited beliefs. Kali may want a softer or stronger take.
  4. **Wheel of Fortune (10):** steered away from luck and fate and toward "what's in your hands." No use of "destiny."
  5. **The Magician (1):** often tied to "manifesting" on TikTok. That word is banned, so the script frames the card as skill and using what you have.
  6. **The Empress (3) and The Emperor (4):** mother/fertility and father associations are left out on purpose, to avoid health or family assumptions. Kali can add them back.
  7. **The High Priestess (2):** framed with the brand's discernment pillar (fear, ego, desire or intuition). Words like "psychic" are avoided.
- **Open questions:** deck/artwork; posting order and cadence; whether Kali wants reversed meanings in a later series.
- **Facts needing verification:** nothing is stated as fact beyond widely known card symbolism. Imagery is hedged with "often shown as" because the deck isn't chosen yet.
- **Assumptions from the brief this draft leans on (and how the copy handles each):**
  - Audience pain "unsure whether a strong feeling is fear, ego, desire or intuition" [inference]: used only in The High Priestess script as a general possibility ("a strong feeling can be..."), not stated as something the viewer feels.
  - No other audience pains are stated. The Chariot line about feeling stuck is framed as something that "can" happen, not as a claim about viewers.
  - Kali's "not predicting the future" stance [src: kalimeister.com/about]: shapes every script but is not quoted. The verbatim line contains an em dash, and the brief allows a paraphrase only.
  - "I read it as..." lines put interpretation in Kali's voice. They describe a reading approach, not her experience, credentials or clients. She should rewrite any that don't match how she reads.
  - Facebook captions are written in first person without claiming experiences or stories.
