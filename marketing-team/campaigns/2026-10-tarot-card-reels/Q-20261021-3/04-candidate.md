# Major Arcana script pack: cards 11-21 (Justice to The World), r2

## Series notes

**Shared structure (every video, same order):**
1. HOOK, spoken in the first 2 seconds, with the ON-SCREEN TEXT version on screen.
2. SCRIPT: card name and number, what the card often shows, what it traditionally points to, then a reflection framing. Never a prediction.
3. CLOSING QUESTION, spoken and shown on screen to invite comments.

**Length:** Script word counts below are for the SCRIPT body only. Hook plus closing question add roughly 15 to 20 words, so each video runs about 35 to 50 seconds at a relaxed pace. If a take runs long, trim the image description first.

**CTA (no spoken CTA, no booking mention, no prices in the videos):**
- TikTok captions end "Free guide in bio."
- Facebook captions end "Free guide at kalimeister.com/letters"
- YouTube Shorts descriptions end "Free guide: kalimeister.com/letters"

**For Kali:**
- The framing follows your About page stance that tarot is about looking at the present, not predicting the future. Every card is written as a question about now.
- **Imagery lines:** The "often shown as..." descriptions follow the most widely known versions of each card, which mostly come from one tradition. Before filming, adjust each imagery line to match the deck you're holding. Cut symbols your card doesn't show and name the ones it does.
- A few scripts use "I read it as..." in your voice (Justice, Tower, Star). Please change those to how you actually read the card.
- Death, the Devil and the Tower are written to be calm. Please keep replies in the comments in the same tone, with no "this means X will happen" and no "nothing bad will happen" either.
- Reversed meanings aren't covered. That could be its own series later.

## 11. Justice

**Working title:** Why Justice holds both a sword and scales

**HOOK:** "Justice holds a sword and a set of scales. Why both?"

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Justice: sword and scales. Why both?

**SCRIPT (85 words):**
This is Justice, card eleven. It's often shown as a figure seated between two pillars, holding scales in one hand and an upright sword in the other. The scales are about weighing things fairly. The sword is about cutting through to what's true. Traditionally, this card points to honesty, fairness and cause and effect, meaning what we chose and what followed. I read it as an invitation to look at a situation honestly, your own part included, and to go easy on yourself while you do.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** If you weighed this situation fairly, what would you see?

**SHOT NOTES:** Kali to camera, holding up or cutting to the Justice card (artwork TBD) when she names the scales and sword. Adjust the imagery line to the deck.

**Working title:** The card that's upside down on purpose

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Hanged Man is upside down on purpose

**SCRIPT (82 words):**
The Hanged Man, card twelve. The figure is usually suspended upside down by one foot, and the face is often calm. Some decks show a soft glow around the head. Traditionally, this card points to a pause. Waiting, letting go of the push, and seeing things from a different angle. The figure has stopped struggling, and from there it may notice things you miss when you're rushing. If things feel stuck, this card asks whether the stillness has something to show you.

## 13. Death

**Working title:** The Death card, calmly

**HOOK:** "Pulled the Death card? Let's look at it calmly."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Pulled Death? Let's look at it calmly.

**SCRIPT (95 words):**
Death is card thirteen, and it has a scary name for a fairly gentle idea. In tarot it's a symbol, and it points to endings and change, one chapter closing so another can begin. It's often shown with a rider on a white horse, and in many decks there's a sun rising in the distance. The rising sun is the other half of the meaning, something new starting as something finishes. The card is a calm question about what has already run its course. A role, a habit, a story you've been telling about yourself.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What habit, role or story feels ready to close?

**SHOT NOTES:** Soft, even light; Kali seated and relaxed, speaking slowly. Show the card (artwork TBD) after the hook, with no dark filters, dramatic sound or zoom. Adjust the rider and sunrise line to the deck.

This is Temperance, card fourteen. It's often shown as a winged figure pouring water from one cup into another, with one foot on land and one in a stream. Pouring between cups is slow work. Rush it and you spill. Its traditional meanings are balance, patience and blending things that seem opposite, like rest and effort, or what you want and what's practical. **Working title:** Look at the chains

**HOOK:** "Look closely at the chains on the Devil card."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Look closely at the chains.

**SCRIPT (82 words):**
The Devil is card fifteen, and the name sounds heavier than the meaning. It's often shown as a horned figure above two people who are chained. In many decks, the chains are loose. Loose enough to lift off. The traditional reading is about the things we feel stuck to, like habits, patterns, comforts, or beliefs about ourselves that we've stopped questioning. It's an honest look at what holds you in place, and whether some of that hold is looser than it feels.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What's one habit you could loosen this week, even a little?

**Working title:** The Tower's quiet question

**HOOK:** "The Tower looks dramatic. Its question is a quiet one."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Tower: dramatic card, quiet question

**SCRIPT (91 words):**
The Tower is card sixteen. It's often shown as a tall tower struck by lightning, with the top breaking away. It's the card people flinch at, so let's slow down. Traditionally, the Tower points to sudden change, especially to something that was built on shaky ground to begin with. A plan, an assumption, a way of seeing things. ## 18. The Moon

**Working title:** When nothing looks clear

**HOOK:** "The Moon card is for when nothing looks clear."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Moon: when nothing looks clear

**SCRIPT (96 words):**
The Moon is card eighteen. It's often shown as a moon above a winding path, with a dog and a wolf on either side and a crayfish at the water's edge. Everything is lit, but only partly. Traditionally, it's linked to uncertainty, imagination and the things we can't see clearly yet. It's the card of "I'm not sure what's real here." That's where discernment comes in. A feeling can be fear, or it can be intuition, and in moonlight they look alike. The Moon asks you to notice a strong feeling before you act on it.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What strong feeling would you like to look at again in daylight?

**SHOT NOTES:** Dim, warm light. Card shown (artwork TBD) while she names the path, dog, wolf and crayfish; cut any symbol the chosen deck doesn't have.
