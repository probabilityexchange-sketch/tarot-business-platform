# Major Arcana script pack: cards 11-21 (Justice to The World)

## Series notes

**Shared structure (every video, same order):**
1. HOOK, spoken in the first 2 seconds, with the ON-SCREEN TEXT version on screen.
2. SCRIPT: card name and number, what the card often shows, what it traditionally points to, then a reflection framing. Never a prediction.
3. CLOSING QUESTION, spoken and shown on screen to invite comments.

**Length:** Script word counts below are for the SCRIPT body only. Hook plus closing question add roughly 15 to 20 words, so each video runs about 35 to 50 seconds at a relaxed pace. If a take runs long, trim the image description first.

**CTA:** Captions end with "Free guide in bio." (the free three-part discernment guide at kalimeister.com/letters). No spoken CTA, no booking mention, no prices in the videos. On YouTube Shorts, put "Free guide in bio." in the description, since the title has no room.

**For Kali:**
- The framing follows your About page stance that tarot is about looking at the present, not predicting the future. Every card is written as a question about now.
- Card descriptions use "often shown as" and "in many decks" because the artwork isn't chosen yet. Once you pick a deck, check each description against your card and cut anything that doesn't match.
- A few scripts use "I read it as..." in your voice (Justice, Tower, Star). Please change those to how you actually read the card.
- Death, the Devil and the Tower are written to be calm. Please keep replies in the comments in the same tone, with no "this means X will happen."
- Reversed meanings aren't covered. That could be its own series later.

## 11. Justice

**Working title:** Why Justice holds both a sword and scales

**HOOK:** "Justice holds a sword and a set of scales. Why both?"

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Justice: sword and scales. Why both?

**SCRIPT (91 words):**
This is Justice, card eleven. It's often shown as a figure seated between two pillars, holding scales in one hand and an upright sword in the other. The scales are about weighing things fairly. The sword is about cutting through to what's true. Traditionally, this card points to honesty, fairness and cause and effect: what we chose, and what followed. I don't read it as a verdict on you. I read it as an invitation to look at a situation honestly, your own part included, without beating yourself up about it.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** If you weighed this situation fairly, what would you see?

**SHOT NOTES:** Kali to camera, holding up or cutting to the Justice card (artwork TBD) when she names the scales and sword. Steady framing, no music swell.

## 12. The Hanged Man

**Working title:** The card that's upside down on purpose

**HOOK:** "This card is upside down on purpose."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Hanged Man is upside down on purpose

**SCRIPT (93 words):**
The Hanged Man, card twelve. The figure is usually suspended upside down by one foot, and here's the surprising part: the face is often calm. Some decks even show a soft glow around the head. Traditionally, this card points to a pause. Waiting, letting go of the push, and seeing things from a different angle. It's not a punishment. It's more like choosing to stop struggling long enough to notice something you couldn't see while you were rushing. If things feel stuck, this card asks whether the stillness has something to show you.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What might look different if you stopped pushing for a moment?

**SHOT NOTES:** Kali to camera; optional slow rotate of the card (artwork TBD) from upside down to upright as she says "different angle."

## 13. Death

**Working title:** The Death card, calmly

**HOOK:** "Pulled the Death card? Take a breath with me."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Pulled Death? Take a breath.

**SCRIPT (100 words):**
Death is card thirteen, and it has a scary name for a fairly gentle idea. In tarot, it's read as a symbol, not a literal event. It points to endings and change: one chapter closing so another can begin. It's often shown with a rider on a white horse, and in many decks there's a sun rising in the distance. That sunrise matters. Something is finishing, and something new is starting. So this card isn't something to fear. It's a calm question about what has already run its course. A role, a habit, a story you've been telling about yourself.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What habit, role or story feels ready to close?

**SHOT NOTES:** Soft, even light; Kali seated and relaxed, speaking slowly. Show the card (artwork TBD) after the hook, not before it, and no dark filters, dramatic sound or zoom.

**TIKTOK CAPTION:** The Death card, calmly: endings, change and a chapter closing. Free guide in bio. #tarot #deathcard #majorarcana #tarottok

**YOUTUBE SHORTS TITLE:** Death tarot card meaning: endings and change, explained calmly

**FACEBOOK CAPTION:** The Death card has a scary name and a calm meaning: endings, change and room for something new. Free guide in bio.

## 14. Temperance

**Working title:** Temperance and the slow pour

**HOOK:** "Two cups, one stream of water, and a lot of patience."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Temperance: two cups, one stream, patience

**SCRIPT (85 words):**
This is Temperance, card fourteen. It's often shown as a winged figure pouring water from one cup into another, with one foot on land and one in a stream. Pouring between cups is slow work. Rush it and you spill. Traditionally, this card points to balance, patience and blending things that seem opposite, like rest and effort, or what you want and what's practical. It comes right after Death in the sequence. After an ending, there's often a quieter stretch of finding a new mix.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What are you trying to balance right now, and where are you rushing it?

## 15. The Devil

**Working title:** Look at the chains

**HOOK:** "Look closely at the chains on the Devil card."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** Look closely at the chains.

**SCRIPT (92 words):**
The Devil is card fifteen, and the name sounds heavier than the meaning. It's often shown as a horned figure above two people who are chained. In many decks, the chains are loose. Loose enough to lift off. Traditionally, this card points to the things we feel stuck to: habits, patterns, comforts, or beliefs about ourselves that we've stopped questioning. It isn't about evil, and it isn't about anyone being bad. It's an honest look at what holds you in place, and whether some of that hold is looser than it feels.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What's one habit you could loosen this week, even a little?

**SHOT NOTES:** Calm, bright setting. If the chosen deck shows loose chains, close-up on that detail during "Loose enough to lift off"; if not, cut that line (see Notes for reviewer).

## 16. The Tower

**Working title:** The Tower's quiet question

**HOOK:** "The Tower looks dramatic. Its question is a quiet one."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Tower: dramatic card, quiet question

**SCRIPT (101 words):**
The Tower is card sixteen. It's often shown as a tall tower struck by lightning, with the top breaking away. It's the card people flinch at, so let's slow down. Traditionally, the Tower points to sudden change, especially to something that was built on shaky ground to begin with. A plan, an assumption, a way of seeing things. When that gives way, it can feel abrupt. But the card doesn't say anything bad is coming. I read it as a prompt to look at what you've built and ask which parts are actually solid, and which parts you've been propping up.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What are you holding up by effort alone?

**SCRIPT (89 words):**
This is the Star, card seventeen. It's often shown as a figure kneeling by water under a large bright star, pouring water from two jugs. After the Tower's upheaval, this card is quiet. Traditionally, the Star points to hope, renewal and a sense of direction coming back slowly. Nothing about it is loud. It's like the first clear night after a storm. ## 18. The Moon

**Working title:** When nothing looks clear

**HOOK:** "The Moon card is for when nothing looks clear."

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (hook):** The Moon: when nothing looks clear

**SCRIPT (97 words):**
The Moon is card eighteen. It's often shown as a moon above a winding path, with a dog and a wolf on either side and a crayfish at the water's edge. Everything is lit, but only partly. Traditionally, this card points to uncertainty, imagination and the things we can't see clearly yet. It's the card of "I'm not sure what's real here." That's where discernment comes in. A feeling can be fear, or it can be intuition, and in moonlight they look alike. The Moon asks you to notice a strong feeling before you act on it.

**CLOSING QUESTION:** What strong feeling would you like to look at again in daylight?

**SHOT NOTES:** Dim, warm light. Card shown (artwork TBD) while she names the path, dog, wolf and crayfish; cut any symbol the chosen deck doesn't have.
