Eyebrow: ON DISCERNMENT · LETTER 01

H1: A strong feeling is a place to begin.

Sometimes you feel something before you have words for it.

A hesitation. A pull toward something. A sense that a conversation needs another question.

Then comes the wondering: Is this intuition? Fear? Something I want to believe?

You don't have to answer immediately.

Discernment can begin with giving an impression your attention while leaving its meaning open. Writing offers a place to do that: to notice what you felt before the explanations begin.

[A MOMENT ON THE PAGE]
Try finishing these four sentences:
What I noticed was…
The meaning I gave it was…
What I hope or fear is…
What I can actually check is…

Keep your first observation separate from your interpretation. "My stomach tightened" and "This person is dishonest" are different statements. The first describes an experience; the second needs examination.

[Pull quote] You can listen to yourself and still ask questions.

For this week, choose one small impression. Write it down, leave room for uncertainty, and return to it later.

What do you notice when you give yourself that space? You're welcome to reply.

Kali
