H1: A boundary can be an act of discernment.

Sometimes the question 'Is this intuition or fear?' keeps us searching for a certainty we don't yet have.

Meanwhile, a simpler question may be available: What am I willing and able to offer?

Perhaps you need more time before making a commitment. Perhaps you have reached the limit of your energy. Perhaps a request asks for more than you want to give.

You don't need certainty about another person's motives to name your own limits. A boundary can describe your availability without becoming a verdict about someone else.

[A MOMENT ON THE PAGE]
Choose a small request or commitment you're considering:
What is being asked of me?
What do I have the time and energy to offer?
What would I need to agree freely?
What could I say clearly and kindly?

Your answer might sound ordinary: 'I need a day to think about it.' 'I can help with this part.' 'I'm not available this week.'

Notice whether you begin inventing a larger explanation to justify a simple need. Put that explanation on the page, too, and ask what you actually know.

[Pull quote] You can be clear about your limits while remaining uncertain about the story.
