ON DISCERNMENT · LETTER 06

Make room for a listening practice.

Over these letters, we’ve made room for impressions, asked questions of fear and desire, and allowed our understanding to change.

Now we can bring those questions into a practice that is small enough to keep.

Begin with one ordinary situation. Notice what arises. Write before you explain. Name what you want and what concerns you. Then consider what you can check.

A MOMENT ON THE PAGE

Give yourself a few quiet minutes with these questions:

What am I noticing?

What am I hoping for or afraid of?

What do I know, and what remains uncertain?

What small, reversible step could help me learn?

Return to the page later. What did the step reveal? What did you misunderstand? What deserves another question? Look for patterns across several experiences rather than making one moment prove everything.

You don’t have to turn every impression into an answer.

Some impressions ask for attention. Some questions take time. A listening practice can leave room for both.
