What happens when an impression you felt strongly about doesn’t hold up?

It can be tempting to defend it, explain it away, or decide you can never trust yourself again.

There is another possibility: let the experience teach you something.

Discernment leaves room for new information. You can take an impression seriously, examine it, and change your understanding. None of those steps requires you to turn against yourself.

Writing gives you something more useful than a verdict about whether you were right. It gives you a record of what you noticed and what you learned.

Return to one impression you wrote down earlier:

What did I notice at the time?

What did I assume it meant?

What happened, and what is still unknown?

What would I understand differently now?

You may find that you noticed something real and gave it the wrong meaning. You may find that a hope or fear shaped your interpretation. You may simply need more time to know.

Trust can include the freedom to reconsider.

For this week, try replacing ‘I should have known’ with ‘What can I learn from this?’
