ON DISCERNMENT · LETTER 03

What do you need to be true?

Discernment asks for honesty about what we want.

Sometimes an impression arrives alongside a powerful wish: to be chosen, to be safe, to be right, or to believe we already know the answer.

When I use the word “ego” here, I mean the part of us invested in protecting an image of ourselves—the capable one, the helpful one, the one who always knows.

Those wishes deserve compassion. They also deserve a place on the page where we can see them.

A MOMENT ON THE PAGE

Choose a question you’re sitting with and write:

What do I want the answer to be?

What would that answer say about me?

What evidence might change my mind?

What remains uncertain?

You can want something deeply and still examine your impressions about it.

An intuitive impression doesn’t have to become a declaration. You can hold it as a possibility, look for evidence, and revise your understanding as you learn.

Try this sentence:

I’m noticing something, and I’m still learning what it means.

Does that feel freeing, uncomfortable, or both? I’d welcome your reply.
